Cohasset MN real estate runs 10 to 15 percent cheaper than comparable homes in Grand Rapids, and it's five to seven minutes down the road. That gap is the whole story for a lot of buyers, and it's worth understanding before you write it off as "just a suburb of Grand Rapids."

I'm Malcolm Wallaker, a licensed REALTOR with Pemberton Real Estate based in Grand Rapids. Cohasset is inside my regular working area, not a place I drive past. Here's what actually matters if you're looking here.

## Why Buyers Look at Cohasset Instead of Grand Rapids

Cohasset is a small city of roughly 2,600 people sitting just west of Grand Rapids, close enough that most residents treat the two towns as one commute. You get Grand Rapids' jobs, schools, and hospital without paying Grand Rapids prices for the house.

Buyers who choose Cohasset over Grand Rapids are usually doing simple math: same commute, same school district in most cases, lower purchase price. For a family stretching a budget, that difference can mean the gap between a fixer and a home that's actually move-in ready.

The tradeoff is fewer walkable amenities. Cohasset is residential and quiet. If you want to walk to a coffee shop or the river, Grand Rapids proper still wins. If you want a driveway, a yard, and a shorter mortgage, Cohasset is the better play.

## What Cohasset Home Prices Actually Look Like

Non-lakefront homes in Cohasset typically run $150,000 to $280,000, depending on age, lot size, and condition. That's meaningfully below Grand Rapids' in-town range, where comparable homes often start closer to $180,000 and climb past $300,000 for anything updated.

Newer construction and larger acreage lots push toward $350,000 and up, but you're still buying more land for the money than you would find in Grand Rapids proper. Older homes from the 1960s through 1980s make up a good share of the inventory, so budget for some updating on anything under $200,000.

## Pokegama Lake Access and What It's Really Worth

Pokegama Lake touches Cohasset, and that's the draw for a chunk of buyers who ask about the town by name. But lakefront here is its own pricing tier, separate from the town's general affordability, and it's easy to confuse the two.

A modest lot with lake frontage on Pokegama starts well above the non-lakefront range, often $300,000 and up depending on frontage and shoreline condition. "Near the lake" and "on the lake" are not the same thing, and I've seen buyers get excited about a listing's proximity to Pokegama only to find out the lot itself has no frontage or deeded access. Read the listing language carefully, or better, have someone who knows the shoreline pull the plat for you.

## Commuting into Grand Rapids: What to Expect

The drive from most of Cohasset into downtown Grand Rapids runs five to ten minutes depending on where you land in town. That's not a real commute by most standards, it's closer to driving across a neighborhood.

Winter driving is the only real variable. Cohasset roads get plowed on a reasonable schedule, but if you're working an early shift at the hospital or need to be at the school by a set time, build in a few extra minutes during a heavy snow month. This isn't a Cohasset-specific problem, it's a northern Minnesota problem, but it's worth factoring in when you're comparing an in-town Grand Rapids commute of zero minutes to a Cohasset commute of five to ten.

## Who Cohasset Actually Fits

Cohasset works well for families who want the Grand Rapids school system and job market without the Grand Rapids price tag. It also works for buyers who want a quieter, more residential setting and don't need to walk to downtown businesses.

It's a tougher fit for buyers who specifically want walkability, or for anyone chasing a true lakefront property on a budget, since Pokegama frontage prices the same as lakefront anywhere else in Itasca County. Know which of those two buyers you are before you start touring.
